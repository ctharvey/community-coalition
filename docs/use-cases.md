# Example workflows

**Status:** Illustrations for requirements and testing; none is a confirmed event.

## UC-01: Brookfield + Wieland PTO neighborhood garage sale

**Concept:** Hold a neighborhood garage sale in the elementary-school parking lot. Brookfield organizes neighborhood participation; Wieland Elementary PTO could sell food and drinks for its own fundraising if it chooses.

### Independent approval steps

1. Brookfield board/committee determines whether sponsoring or supporting the event is within HOA authority and insurance coverage.
2. PTO leadership determines whether to participate and confirms its own fundraising responsibilities.
3. PTO obtains all necessary PfISD facility and vendor/food permissions and verifies district requirements. No event is represented as approved before that process finishes.
4. Each organization approves any official listing of itself as a host or co-host.
5. Organizer group shares a checklist, traffic/parking plan, weather contingencies, and equipment requests.
6. Public calendar lists the event only after required approvals; volunteers sign up via an ad-free external link.
7. PTO revenue, if any, goes through the PTO's own financial arrangements, not through Brookfield or coalition funds.
8. Save vetted checklists and lessons learned for another organization's future event.

## UC-02: Community fall festival staffed by volunteers

- An HOA creates the public event, then creates tasks such as setup, cleanup, greeting, and supply delivery.
- Neighbors can claim individual slots without a Hylo account using an OpenSignup-style public page.
- Organizer admins can see their own roster; unrelated PTO organizers cannot access personal details.
- If more elaborate shifts, qualifications, and hour tracking become necessary, evaluate Ephios.
- Volunteers requiring school clearance still follow the school's separate process.

## UC-03: Resident discovers nearby events

- A resident sees approved events from HOAs, schools, MUDs, nonprofits, and apartments in one public list.
- Every event displays the true host, original source, current status, time zone, and location.
- If an imported source cancels an event, the aggregator updates or withdraws the listing.
- Private school planning events must never appear.
- Organizations with no machine-readable feed can submit events to a curator for review.

## UC-04: A PTO sells paid event tickets

- An authorized PTO officer connects the PTO's own Stripe account through Stripe's onboarding.
- PTO creates its own products/tickets in a platform such as Hi.Events.
- Purchasers pay the PTO as seller; the transaction is a direct charge on the connected account, not money held for redistribution by the coalition.
- PTO manages refunds, taxes, donation language, fulfillment, and reconciliation.
- A buyer can pay for one organization's products in one checkout. Cross-organization carts are deferred.

## UC-05: Leadership changes

- PTO president or HOA social chair rotates out.
- Governing organization designates and verifies successors through its own process.
- Platform admin access can be transferred/revoked without changing who legally owns the content or financial account.
- Activity audit/log and published contact corrections remain available.

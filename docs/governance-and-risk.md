# Governance, legal authority, and risk checklist

**Purpose:** Flag topics for participating boards, district personnel, insurers, and qualified Texas counsel. This document is not legal advice or a finding that any proposed event is authorized.

## Coalition status and authority

- Working coalition concept only; no new nonprofit formed or adopted legal structure.
- No member, HOA board director, PTO officer, MUD representative, or school employee can bind a different organization without proper delegated authority.
- A listing, linked logo, or shared announcement must distinguish `host`, `co-host`, `supporter`, and `listed by aggregator`.
- Organizations should approve final publicity that uses their name, logo, property, staff, or resources.
- If the coalition becomes a public service operator, identify a stable legal operator, terms/privacy notice, responsible admin, funding source, insurance, and succession plan. Whether to incorporate remains open.

## HOA-specific questions

- Does the governing instrument permit the activity, expense, shared equipment, or use of HOA facilities? What board approval and meeting procedures apply?
- Is the event reasonably tied to HOA purposes and benefits? Are HOA assessments and volunteers being used for a non-HOA fundraising activity?
- Whose insurer responds to injuries, damaged property, vendors, volunteers, traffic, or borrowed equipment?
- No assumption that a Brookfield board member speaking here has approval from the full board.

## PTO / PfISD-specific questions

- PTO's organization, EIN, tax exemption, insurance, fundraising practices, and authorized bank account are **unverified**.
- The PTO would handle applications and contact with PfISD for any school-property use; actual written approval, terms, scheduling, indemnities, supervision, food/service approvals, and vendor requirements must be confirmed.
- A school parking-lot garage sale is only an idea until facility permission is granted.
- School-required volunteer screening, access controls, child safety, and student privacy cannot be replaced by our signup app.

## MUD and other public entities

- Public entities may have more limited statutory authority, public-resource constraints, records obligations, publicity rules, and approval procedures. Do not treat MUD participation as equivalent to private HOA participation.
- Do not imply governmental sponsorship or MUD approval by calendar listing alone.

## Payments and fundraising

- **Direct charges only** to each organization's authorized Stripe connected account initially; no pooling, pass-through custody, or coalition-issued tax receipts.
- Confirm each organization's legal identity and authority to accept payments, sale/fundraising rules, sales-tax obligations, refund policy, and donor claims.
- Stripe KYC/payment enablement is not IRS tax-exempt recognition. Do not present a donation as deductible until the recipient's eligibility and receipt process are verified.
- Reconcile event/order records with each organization's own payment records; use distinct seller names and statements.
- Credit-card fees remain even when software is free. Platform fee economics and liability must be reviewed against the exact Connect configuration.

## Privacy, safety, and security

- Public calendar includes approved event metadata only.
- Private organizer discussions, school/student information, personal volunteer details, and internal permission documents stay out of public search and exports.
- Do not collect unnecessary birthdates, addresses, children's identities, background-check files, bank credentials, or identification documents.
- Enforce strict per-organization data isolation, least-privilege roles, two-factor authentication for payment administrators, and auditable leadership handoffs.
- Publish plain-language privacy policy, retention schedule, consent for reminders, reporting channel, and deletion/export process before collecting personal data.
- Make public event feeds, webhooks, and importers resilient to fraudulent listings, spam, SSRF, forged callbacks, duplicates, and stale/canceled events.

## OSS licensing

- Apache-2.0 and MIT projects are generally easier to combine under their terms.
- AGPL projects such as OpenSignup and Hi.Events carry source-availability obligations for modifications offered over a network; Hi.Events also states specific attribution terms. Review precise licenses before incorporating code; linking separately deployed systems is usually simpler.
- Do not add an OSS license to this repository until the project's intended code and license have been discussed.

## Minimum event collaboration agreement topics

Who is authorized? What is the event and facility? Who holds permits/insurance? Who is responsible for vendors, sales, volunteers, child safety, marketing, cancellation, incident response, and expenses? How do parties withdraw or correct public attribution?
